## Scope and authority

This policy covers Junction-controlled account, Vendor, Staff, marketplace, media, engagement, transactional, support, security, provider, and operational records. It includes PostgreSQL, authentication stores, object versions, projections, queues, caches, logs, exports, local fixtures, backups, WAL, archives, and copies held by contracted processors. Vendor-controlled independent records require a separately documented responsibility boundary.

Once adopted, this document governs purpose, schedule approval, exceptions, and deletion evidence. [Data classification](./DATA-CLASSIFICATION-RETENTION-EXPORT-AND-DELETION.md) governs handling classes. [Rejected-media operations](./REJECTED-MEDIA-RETENTION.md) owns the implemented media timer, hold API, retries, journal, and reconciliation details. Any inconsistency must be resolved through a versioned decision before automation changes. Existing deadlines and active holds continue to follow their stored policy versions.

The future Dire Dawa market context and the user's timezone do not establish applicable law. Determine jurisdiction from the operator, people served, processing activities, storage/processor locations, and applicable legal scope. Retention minima, erasure deadlines, exceptional-content rules, and processor duties require current jurisdiction-specific review under the [regulatory validation gate](../future/REGULATORY-PAYMENT-TAX-KYB-AND-INVOICING-VALIDATION.md).

## Governing rules

1. Collect and retain only fields needed for a documented purpose. Public publication, encryption, append-only storage, or possible future usefulness does not justify indefinite retention.
2. Assign each record class a versioned schedule before collecting real data. An unresolved schedule blocks release of that class; it is not permission for permanent storage or blanket deletion of existing records.
3. Record the purpose, business rationale, any validated legal basis/minimum/maximum, accountable owner, trigger, duration, deletion action, hold behavior, processor propagation, backup maximum, approval, effective date, and verification reference. Split fields with different purposes instead of extending a whole record to the longest period.
4. A legal minimum applies only to the specific facts and fields the reviewed obligation covers. Resolve conflicts with deletion requirements through a documented legal decision. Preserve evidence under a scoped hold while that decision is pending when preservation is permitted.
5. Restrict access immediately when entitlement or publication ends. Access revocation and physical erasure are distinct events. Keep retired evidence private, with case-scoped access and an audit trail.
6. Use UTC instants. Unless stated otherwise, a day means 24 elapsed hours. The retention trigger must be an authoritative business event; reads, backup copies, restores, and routine retries do not reset it. A new purpose or extension requires a new recorded decision.
7. Pseudonymized identifiers, checksums, object keys, and linked audit records remain subject to a schedule. Claim anonymization only after assessing whether people can still be identified through retained links or other available data.
8. Track live-store deletion, derived-store propagation, processor confirmation, and archive expiry separately. Report completion only for the scope actually verified. A failed job or locked object remains an open obligation.

## Preservation holds and exceptional disposal

A hold preserves specifically identified records for an active appeal, dispute, support/security investigation, or reviewed legal preservation duty. Record case/reference, owner, reason, scope/fields/stores, authorization, start time, next review date and release decision. A Listing reference or an open-ended note alone is not a sufficient governance record.

The proposed review cadence is every 30 days and whenever the case resolves or the governing obligation changes. Review overdue holds with the accountable owner; never release them automatically. Avoid copying an entire account or backup when a narrow evidence copy suffices. Multiple preservation reasons must all be resolved before release. Exceptional content requiring earlier disposal or restricted preservation needs prompt review under applicable law, with a recorded decision and safe handling path.

The current media API offers one held/unheld flag with a reason, scoped reviewer permission and audit, rather than a multi-case hold registry or review scheduler. Operators must reconcile all active preservation reasons before using release. The media release grace remains governed by `rejected-media-v1`. Other classes use their approved remaining period or explicit grace; the media grace must not silently become a universal rule.

For media, the independent journal marks the irreversible deletion decision. A hold can prevent deletion only before that entry is committed; it cannot undo journaled deletion, even after a partial failure. Escalate a late preservation request with the known deletion status; never claim the data can be recovered or revive it from a backup.

## Deletion, requests and evidence

Routine expiry and verified deletion requests share a scoped disposal workflow. Applicable law may require earlier action than the routine schedule. Authenticate the requester proportionately, establish ownership/scope, revoke applicable access, check specific holds/obligations, and record any retained fields and reason. The project must set response deadlines from the selected jurisdiction; this draft does not import a foreign statutory deadline.

Delete or appropriately anonymize eligible authoritative fields, object versions, temporary files, projections, caches, exports, queued payloads and processor copies. Retain another party's required transaction facts only under the validated schedule and access restriction. Send processor deletion instructions where the responsibility/contract requires them and track acknowledgments; an API request alone does not prove provider deletion.

Record policy version, trigger/due time, scoped identifiers, job/attempt, affected stores, holds checked, outcome, verification time and remaining obligations. Keep evidence minimal; do not duplicate source content or secrets in logs. A deletion status must distinguish access revoked, live stores purged, processor action pending, archives restricted pending expiry, and all recoverable copies verified gone. Notify the requester accurately about retained fields and residual archive deadlines.

## Backups, Object Lock and restoration

The proposed 35-day ceiling applies to the oldest recoverable data, not merely the latest backup filename. Copying, re-encryption, restore/export, or repackaging must not renew a generation's expiry. Preserve dependency integrity while rotating full/incremental backups and WAL. If dependencies or locks make the ceiling impossible, resolve the conflict before selecting the storage configuration; record existing exceptions with their expiry and remediation owner.

Keep inaccessible archives exclusively for recovery until the approved expiry; prevent ordinary browsing, processing and public exposure. Verify physical disposal after lock/lifecycle expiry. Reconcile deletion records into a fenced restore before projections or application access resume. The independent current journal must outlive all recovery points that could recreate deleted media; restoring an older journal is insufficient.

Object Lock is not an erasure mechanism: Amazon S3 documents that compliance-mode retention cannot be shortened and locked versions cannot be deleted during the period. This is a technical benchmark only; validate equivalent behavior in the selected MinIO/B2 service. [AWS Object Lock documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/object-lock.html), retrieved 2026-10-09 (`REF-034`).

The existing local tools reconcile version-preserving media copies and restored database rows. The [media backup lifecycle](./MEDIA-BACKUP-LIFECYCLE.md) now schedules encrypted media generations and expires registered standalone archives locally, including retries after Object Lock expiry. It does not manage pgBackRest incremental/WAL dependencies, arbitrary historical layouts, or activate offsite services. Non-media classes need their own durable deletion/restore controls before claiming that an account deletion survives recovery. A recovery retention limit does not satisfy a longer finance retention duty; preserve required finance facts in the approved authoritative archive rather than retaining every personal-data backup indefinitely.

## Research basis and limits

The UK ICO guidance is an authoritative design reference, not a finding that UK law governs Junction. Its storage-limitation guidance supports purpose-based, justified schedules, periodic review, disposal, and distinguishing pseudonymization from anonymization; it does not prescribe Junction's numeric periods. The page reports that it is under review following UK legislative changes. [ICO storage limitation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/storage-limitation/), retrieved 2026-10-09 (`REF-032`).

ICO erasure guidance addresses backup copies, transparency about residual retention and keeping pending-expiry copies beyond ordinary use. This informs the proposed archive controls; it does not establish that delayed backup erasure is lawful in Junction's eventual jurisdiction. [ICO right to erasure, backup systems](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/right-to-erasure/), retrieved 2026-10-09 (`REF-033`).

The [global source register](../governance/AUTHORITATIVE-SOURCE-REGISTER.md) records these sources and refresh triggers. No selected-jurisdiction legal opinion or financial recordkeeping period was established by this investigation.
